# Competitive Landscape — Research Matrix
_As of 2026-10-15. Status: qualitative research shortlist; no market-share claims._

| Category | Examples to investigate | User value being tested | USKORUN differentiation hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Social running networks | Strava | Feed, activity tracking, clubs, community | Offline, merchant-funded reward redemption |
| Free training ecosystem | Nike Run Club | Guided runs, motivation | Club-to-merchant conversion |
| Device ecosystems | Garmin Connect | Trusted GPS / wearables | Cross-device challenges without device lock-in |
| Coaching platforms | Runna | Structured personal plan | Local partner and group discovery |
| Activity logging | ASICS Runkeeper, MapMyRun | Track progress | Not competing on maps, paces, training analytics |
| Virtual challenge products | The Conqueror | Distance goals / collectible prizes | Neighborhood merchant economics vs shipped medals |
| Step-based incentives | Sweatcoin, StepBet | Rewards and commitment | Actual social running + merchant check-in |
| Charity activity | Charity Miles | Supporting a cause | Real-world clubs, transparent local sponsorship |
| Health incentive schemes | Paceline, Vitality-style programs | Wellness rewards | Merchant-level control and settlement |
| Offline events | Parkrun-style free 5K events, retail run clubs | Habit + human community | White-label partner infrastructure |

**Important:** Example presence in a category is not a claim that it currently operates the described program in every U.S. location, or that its reward policies are compatible with USKORUN.

## Diligence scorecard (0–5 only when verified)
- Presence and reach in U.S.
- Current paid/free model and published prices
- Source of acquisition (communities, creators, app store, partners, paid)
- Paid conversion/retention and evidence provenance
- Exact reward mechanism, sponsor economics and redemption quality
- Anti-fraud and platform-policy dependence
- Publicly supported strengths/weaknesses (with dated links)

## Immediate blockers to assess
- Existing fitness social graphs have high switching costs.
- Merchant value might be poor if runners would buy anyway.
- API restrictions may prohibit virtual-competition patterns.
- Users may dislike tracking or reporting precise physical activity.
- Rewards can attract fraud and coupon-only consumers.

## Field positioning test
Randomize messaging, not benefit eligibility:
A. “Run 5K, unlock local perks.”
B. “Run together, support local businesses.”
C. “Your club run unlocks member offers.”
Compare verified join rate, actual show-up, redemption and club referral.
